# Gag garden

> A plot of **seeds**, not a menu. Nothing here is approved, tested or required. Grow what delights you; pull out what doesn't. **Related:** [storytelling & humor](../foundations/storytelling-and-humor.md) · [identity](identity.md)

## An optional game: Clawd's rulebook, Road Runner style

Chuck Jones gave the Road Runner rules so every new gag would be legible. A tiny rulebook can do the same for Clawd *if you want to play that game*. These are suggestions to argue with; breaking one on purpose is often the best gag.

1. Clawd never speaks; the status text speaks.
2. The eyes are the emotion channel; the mouth is a rare exception.
3. The claws are the verbs (tap, snip, wave, hold, lift).
4. Clawd is humiliated, never hurt.
5. A small prop set, recombined (cup, laptop, sparkle, zZz, flag, bubble, hat).
6. Calm face, eventful world: the eyes are the last thing to change.
7. One idea per frame.

## Seeds

Each is 2-4 beats, **my proposals, untested**. They come from the research ideas, not from any approved design.

- **The coyote hover (waiting on a slow thing).** Clawd steps off the edge of his cell, hangs in the air for two or three beats, eyes look down, then at us, then a one-pixel drop and a shrug.
- **The empty cup (cache nearly out).** Clawd looks into the mug, tilts it, one eye squints. The prop tells the story.
- **Rule of three (typing).** Claws tap twice in rhythm; the third tap hits something else or misses; eyes widen for one beat.
- **Deadpan confetti (done).** A pause, two small claw raises, then eyes return to neutral as a single confetti pixel lands on his head.
- **The sideways surprise (bored idle).** Counts something, sidles to the edge, peeks back. Walking forward would be the surprise.
- **Notice, pause, react.** Eyes shift, hold one beat, then claws. Works with almost any new event.
- **Sad Mac energy (error).** One change: eyes to flat lines, a tilted claw, a tiny prop. No long performance.
- **Boredom-as-verb.** Verbs for a crab: sidle, snip, peek, burrow, scuttle, tap, flinch, preen, bubble, juggle, nap. Pick one at random and ask what the prop would be.
- **A dream flicker (sleeping).** The twitching claw and the peeking eye already exist; what is he dreaming about? A zZz that briefly becomes a tiny sparkle?
- **A rare variant every Nth loop.** Any idle can hide a one-in-twenty surprise.

## Ways to find your own

1. **Ask "what would it do if it were bored?"** Then "if it were smug?", "if it were late?", "if it were on holiday?".
2. **Mine the crab.** Sideways walking, snapping claws, shells, tide pools, hermit moves, molting.
3. **Mine the work.** Compiling, waiting on a test, merge conflicts, a flaky CI, a very long diff.
4. **Mine the constraint.** The grid itself can be part of the joke (a pixel that doesn't belong, a claw that steps outside his cell).
5. **Steal the shape, not the surface.** Take the *structure* of a joke from a silent comedian or a cartoon and rebuild it in rectangles.
6. **Play at the wrong size.** Draw it huge on paper, then crush it into the grid; or draw it in 8x8 first.
7. **Take something away.** Remove the prop, the eyes, the colour. What survives is the idea.

## What to avoid (gently)

- Jokes at the moment the user needs to act.
- Anything that makes him look in pain rather than embarrassed.
- Gags that only work if you know the lore.
- Making every look a gag. A calm Clawd makes the funny one funnier.
