# Mario, 16x16 (Donkey Kong, 1981)

> **Read when:** you must give a character an identity with almost no pixels. **Template:** constraint → choices → sacrificed → what reads → what to steal (not copy).


## Constraint
A 16x16 sprite on arcade hardware with about three animation frames. In Miyamoto's account (Iwata Asks), a realistic human with a face wouldn't fit: stretch the body and almost nothing is left for the head.

## Choices (in his own reasoning, paraphrased)
- **Cap** instead of hair: hair is hard to draw and animate, and the brim lets the eyes sit right under it.
- **Moustache** to suggest nose and mouth at once; the ambiguity is fine because it costs fewer pixels. A secondary account adds that it separates the nose from the face.
- **Overalls over a contrasting shirt** so arm swing is readable in about three frames.
- **White gloves** so hand motion shows in jumps.
- The carpenter job and the Italian identity came later, from setting and context; accounts differ on the order.

## Sacrificed
Realistic proportions, hair, a visible mouth, a range of expressions, finger detail.

## What reads
Colour blocking (red cap, blue overalls, skin face, white hands) turns four small regions into a person. Chunky proportions are a feature.

## What to steal
- For every costly feature you can't draw, find **a one-shape stand-in**, ideally high-contrast.
- Choose features that make **motion** legible, not only the still pose.
- Let the limit become identity; don't "fix" it later.

## Caution
A "retro-replay" article claims the moustache was a pure hardware trick; its attributions could not be verified. Only the Iwata Asks interview is treated as primary here, and Miyamoto's explanations differ between interviews.

## For Clawd
Eyes are the face; claws are the gloves (position and silhouette, since they share the body's colour); hats sit on the head slot like his cap. See [pixel economy](../foundations/pixel-economy.md) and [body language](../foundations/body-language-and-props.md).
