# Susan Kare: the Macintosh icons

> **Read when:** you need to be clear and friendly in a handful of pixels, or are tempted to joke where users are tense. **Template:** constraint → choices → sacrificed → what reads → what to steal (not copy).


## Constraint
1-bit monochrome, a 32x32 icon grid (1,024 squares), no anti-aliasing; fonts on roughly 9x7 cells using only horizontal, vertical and 45-degree strokes.

## Choices
Sketch on graph paper (her roots are needlepoint and mosaic), convert to bits, iterate. Success test: an icon works if you can tell someone what it is once and they don't forget it. Prefer a visual metaphor to a label, think of traffic signs, avoid tying icons to current hardware (floppies age badly). Borrow proven signs: the Command symbol came from Swedish campground signs.

## Humor
Happy Mac and Sad Mac, the bomb, Clarus the Dogcow (a distorted dog-or-cow whose ambiguity made it memorable), a pirate flag with a rainbow-apple eye patch. Humour is placed at **failure states and edge cases**, where users are tense.

## What reads
Few details, strong silhouettes, familiar metaphors.

## A warning
The bomb icon for fatal errors was expected to stay hidden; one owner phoned Apple fearing the machine would explode. **Icons are read literally.**

## What to steal
- **Metaphor over invention**, and borrowed signs over new ones.
- **One-sentence recall test**: "the crab with wide eyes means it needs me."
- **Jokes in failure states**, but never alarming ones when you only mean to inform.
- Author each size separately (Windows later made this official: toolbar icons have less detail; Microsoft Learn, Raymond Chen).

## For Clawd
His face is a Happy Mac: its job is reassurance. Error moods can be funny and sheepish without being frightening. See [storytelling](../foundations/storytelling-and-humor.md).
