# Clippy vs. Desktop Goose

> **Read when:** you are designing when and how often a character speaks up. **Template:** constraint → choices → sacrificed → what reads → what to steal (not copy).


## Constraint
A character that lives on someone's screen while they work.

## Clippy (Microsoft Office Assistant, 1997)
About 250 characters were designed across the Bob/Office project (Atteberry says he contributed 15-20) with Stanford social psychologists over six months; the paperclip tested best. Luke Swartz's study (via summaries): people disliked what Clippy *did*, not what it *was*. It interrupted with etiquette-breaking prompts, had no memory of the user, replaced a smarter learning core with fixed rules, and its idle animations meant to make it feel alive pulled focus. Reeves and Nass's "media equation": a face makes software a social partner, so it was judged like a rude colleague.

## Desktop Goose
Steals your cursor, drags mud, leaves notes and memes; nearly as intrusive as Clippy and loved (4.8/5 from about 2,000 ratings), because users opted in, behaviour is configurable, and there is an off switch.

## BonziBuddy (the extreme)
A companion on Microsoft Agent tech classified as adware; the FTC fined it in 2004 (snippet-level source). A pet that monetises attention poisons the genre.

## What to steal
- **Audit behaviour before looks.** Clippy's drawing was fine.
- **Consent changes the same behaviour** into charm.
- **Failure checklist:** uninvited interruption, stateless and context-blind, idle motion that grabs focus, no dismissal, thin utility so personality is all there is.
- **Do-nothing is a behaviour.** The Mochi pet often chooses not to react, using cooldowns and probabilities.

## For Clawd
Stand-up and water reminders are opt-in-feeling and acknowledgeable; moods hold 2 s before changing; sleeping is honest idle. See [companion presence](../foundations/companion-presence.md).
