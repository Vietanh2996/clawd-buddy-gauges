# Exercises

> **Read when:** you want to get better at *seeing*, not just placing pixels. Do them in a scratch space; keep what teaches you something. Nothing here is a requirement.

## Seeing

1. **The flat-fill gauntlet.** Pick six existing looks. Fill each with one colour at 1x. Rank them by how nameable they are. What did the best ones do? ([silhouette](../foundations/silhouette-and-readability.md))
2. **One-second guess.** Show a friend (or an agent with no context) a look for one second; record the guess. Repeat across fifteen looks. Which were misread, and why?
3. **Greyscale.** Turn a look to greyscale. What disappeared?
4. **Score a loop.** Write any look's frames as a table of beats and ms. Mark the rare event, the blink, the longest hold.
5. **Frame diff.** For one look, compute how many pixels change between frames. Which transitions are 1-2 px? Which are 40+? Which of those did you notice?

## Making (small)

6. **Eight eyes.** Take Clawd's body and draw eight new eye states, each with at most four pixels: sleepy, suspicious, proud, sheepish, startled, bored, tipsy, smug. Which ones can't be done in eyes alone, and what prop fixes them?
7. **Tempo swap.** Take one pose pair and play it at three tempos. Name the mood of each.
8. **Cut a frame.** Take a look, delete the weakest frame, double the survivors. Did it improve or hurt?
9. **Prop diet.** Take a look and replace its prop with a single block of colour. Does the pose still say the verb? Then try the reverse.
10. **The rare variant.** Take an idle and design a one-in-twenty surprise that fits in 6 beats.
11. **Eight by eight.** Draw Clawd in 8x8, then 12x12, then 16x16. What did you keep each time? Which size has the most personality per pixel?

## Making (bigger)

12. **A 3-beat gag.** Setup, reinforce, break, in 3-4 distinct frames. Test it on a stranger.
13. **The state from nothing.** Invent a state the band doesn't have (offline, rate-limited, re-reading, merge conflict). Write the verb, the prop and the quietest version first.
14. **Reverse-engineer a hero.** Take one of the [studies](../studies/README.md), say what the constraint was, then redo the problem for a creature that isn't in the study.
15. **Make an ugly one on purpose.** Draw something that breaks three rules and see what charm you can get out of it. Then ask which of the rules you broke mattered.

## Taking apart (analysis)

16. **Steal the structure, not the surface.** Pick a gag from a silent film or cartoon you like; write its structure in beats; rebuild it as a 3-beat Clawd gag.
17. **Fight the KB.** Find a sentence in these modules you disagree with. Write your counter-argument and, if it's good, edit the module.

## When you're stuck

- Draw it bigger, rougher, sillier. Then crush it.
- Ask what the crab *wants*.
- Walk away and come back; the grid invites over-fixing (Derek Yu).
- Change the question from "how do I draw X?" to "what's the smallest thing that says X?"
